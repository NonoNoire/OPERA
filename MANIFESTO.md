# OPERA Manifesto

OPERA begins from a simple methodological premise: the process of research deserves to be documented with the same seriousness usually reserved for its final results.

In artistic research, knowledge is rarely produced through linear progression alone. It often emerges through repeated gestures, unfinished intuitions, material tests, conceptual detours, doubts, accidents, and retrospective interpretation. OPERA is designed to make these movements traceable without forcing them into premature coherence.

## Principles

### 1. Document the process, not only the result

Research outputs are only the visible surface of a longer intellectual and material process. OPERA treats drafts, observations, experiments, notes, failures, and transitions as part of the research record.

### 2. Every idea has a history

Ideas do not appear fully formed. They are shaped by readings, conversations, images, technical constraints, errors, intuitions, and previous formulations. OPERA aims to preserve this genealogy so that concepts can be reconstructed, questioned, and refined.

### 3. Every observation is data

An observation does not need to be complete in order to be relevant. A fragment, a recurring doubt, a practical difficulty, or a material reaction may become meaningful later. OPERA therefore gives methodological value to small and provisional records.

### 4. Doubt is part of research

Doubt is not a defect in the research process. It is a condition for precision. OPERA treats uncertainty, contradiction, and unresolved questions as active components of inquiry rather than as material to be hidden from the record.

### 5. Reflexivity is part of the method

The researcher is not external to the research process. Choices of language, selection, framing, interpretation, and attention all influence what becomes visible. OPERA therefore includes reflexive documentation as a structural element of the method.

### 6. The diary is an epistemic instrument

The research diary is not merely a personal archive. It is a tool for producing, testing, and reorganising knowledge. In OPERA, daily documentation becomes a way to observe the development of thought over time.

### 7. Structure should support complexity without erasing it

OPERA seeks to provide enough structure to make research traceable, but not so much that complexity is simplified into rigid categories. Its architecture is intended to remain adaptable, revisable, and open to methodological correction.

## Purpose

The purpose of OPERA is not to automate thought or to replace interpretation. Its purpose is to make the conditions of interpretation more visible.

By documenting observations, questions, hypotheses, experiments, reflections, and conceptual links, OPERA seeks to create a durable record of how research knowledge is formed, transformed, and situated.
